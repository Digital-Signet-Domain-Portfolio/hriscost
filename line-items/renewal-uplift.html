HRIS renewal price increases

No vendor on hriscost.com publishes how much it raises prices at renewal. The increase is whatever your contract allows: a fixed annual cap, a cap linked to an inflation index, or no cap at all, in which case the vendor can reprice at renewal. That makes the uplift clause the one line of a 3-year HRIS cost you can fix before you sign. The table below shows what different annual increases do to the same contract, and the ledger lets you set your own.

Worked on BambooHR Core at its published $10 per employee per month, 100 employees: $12,000 in year 1. The increase rates are examples to compare, not vendor figures.

The arithmetic: year 2 = year 1 x (1 + increase), year 3 = year 1 x (1 + increase)². The same multipliers apply to any contract value.

Which contracts carry the most renewal risk

Exposure follows the pricing structure. Where a vendor publishes a list rate (Gusto, BambooHR, Deel, Zoho People), you can see the current price at renewal and compare it with the market, so a steep increase is easy to challenge. Where the price is quote-only and the contract runs several years (Workday, UKG, Paycom, HiBob), the renewal price is negotiated privately and switching is expensive once the system is embedded, so the cap you write into the first contract is your main protection.

Cap structures to ask for

  • A fixed annual cap on the per-employee rate for the full term and the first renewal.
  • An index-linked cap, such as the change in CPI, with a ceiling.
  • A price hold for year 2, with a capped increase after.
  • A rate cap rather than a contract-value cap, so a fall in headcount lowers the bill instead of raising the rate.

What to bring to a renewal negotiation

  • Written quotes from two competing vendors at your current headcount.
  • The published list rates for the vendors that have one (see every published rate).
  • Modules you no longer use and want removed.
  • A longer term offered in exchange for a lower or fixed cap.
